# Interview Findings

Anonymised summaries of the discovery interviews. Each section says who it came from (by role only) and how far it can be trusted. This is evidence, not a decision.

## Department staff (the HOD)

For [issue #5](https://github.com/Dhruvivek/CampusFix/issues/5), using the [HOD interview guide](https://github.com/Dhruvivek/CampusFix/blob/main/docs/questionnaire/hod-interview-guide.md).

**Source:** the HOD's answers, passed on by the author in the author's own words on 2026-10-26. Paraphrased throughout; there are no direct quotes.

### How a repair request is handled today

- A student writes an application. Staff first check by hand whether the problem is real and whether it is serious, or can wait a week or so. This check is described as tiring because the application carries no proof.
- The carpenter is **in-house**.
- Management is informed sometimes, but usually not. No separate spending approval was mentioned.
- **Why it's written:** so both the person who writes the application and the person who receives it are accountable for it.

### Proof

- **A photo or video** is what's wanted. The campus is big and nobody has time to inspect every problem in person.
- **Fake complaints:** small ones are ignored. If one turns out to be a big prank, the student who wrote the application is punished.

### Hearing about new reports

- What they'd want is a **dashboard showing this week's reported problems**.
- Managing many separate applications is hard.
- **Repairs are batched.** The college won't call a carpenter, plumber and so on for every small problem each day. Tradespeople come about **once a week** and fix that week's problems. They are called sooner only for emergencies.

### Status updates

- Willing to update statuses if it takes **one or two clicks**. There's no time to edit details on each problem one at a time.
- Wanted: **update many problems at once**, and/or keep to **two or three simple statuses**. The example given was opening the dashboard and marking "called the carpenter".

### Places and handing off to the carpenter

- Wanted: **filter problems of the same type, then export or print the list** and give it to the carpenter, who works through it one by one.
- **How a location is passed on today:** the HOD sometimes walks to the classroom with the workers and shows them what to check. Otherwise a staff member takes them, or the details are written on paper.
- **Not asked:** whether a list or register of rooms exists.

### Environment

- **College email:** Microsoft.
- **Grievance or complaints portal:** none.

### Not covered in the interview

- Q5: whether a list or register of rooms exists, and how rooms and seats are labelled.
- Q8: a walk-through of the last repair arranged: how they heard, who did what, how long it took, how the student found out.
- Who besides the HOD would update statuses, if anyone.

### Implications to test (author's reading, not the HOD's words)

- A report probably needs photo or video evidence attached to cut down on manual checks.
- The staff view should be a weekly batch view more than a per-report alert stream. That matches how repairs are actually scheduled.
- Status changes need to be bulk and minimal, with very few states.
- A filtered, printable list for each trade (carpenter, plumber, …) may matter more than any notification channel.
- Each report needs a location precise enough for a worker to find on their own. Today that knowledge reaches the worker only if someone walks them there or writes a note.
- A Microsoft email setup suggests Microsoft sign-in is the natural fit if staff or students log in with college accounts.
- With no grievance portal, there's nothing existing to integrate with or compete against.

## Students

For [issue #4](https://github.com/Dhruvivek/CampusFix/issues/4), using the [student interview guide](https://github.com/Dhruvivek/CampusFix/blob/interview-guides/docs/questionnaire/student-interview-guide.md).

### S1: what happened

- **Last broken thing:** 2 of the 5 fans in S1's classroom stopped working within the last month. Effect: it was hot and hard to focus.
- **What S1 did:** S1 and benchmates wrote an application and handed it to the deputy HOD. She said it would be fixed in 2 days, then forgot. About a week later they wrote a second application as a reminder. She apologised and the fans were fixed the next day, so about 8 days in total.
- **Why an application:** "just telling someone about the problem, nobody takes it seriously." Handing it over was "not a big hassle." A written application "means you are not faking it and you are responsible for reporting it."
- **How S1 found out:** saw the fans working again.
- **Reported again later:** yes, a broken water cooler. When S1 handed in the application, the deputy said it had already been reported and would be fixed in 2 days, and it was. S1 hadn't known it was already reported.

### S1: reaction to the idea (opinion, not finding)

- **College email:** a Microsoft account. This matches the HOD's answer. How often S1 uses it was not asked.
- **Name on the report:** "not a big thing, it is a proof that I saw a problem and report it." S1 expressed no worry about blame.
- **Photo:** doesn't mind taking one.
- **First reaction to the idea:** not asked.

### Patterns the author reported across all conversations

The author says most stories were like S1's. These patterns mix the formal interviews with the informal conversations. They aren't attributed to individual students, and how many students mentioned each one is unknown.

- Teachers forget about the problem because of busy schedules.
- The application doesn't reach the right person.
- In most cases, students are afraid to give an application to the HOD or deputy HOD.
- Staff sometimes neglect it and later say they never got the application, or that nobody reported the problem.
- The same problem gets several applications, because students don't know it has already been reported.
