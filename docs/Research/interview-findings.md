# Interview Findings

Anonymised summaries of the discovery interviews. Each section says who it came from (by role only) and how far it can be trusted. This is evidence, not a decision.

## Department staff (the HOD)

For [issue #5](https://github.com/Dhruvivek/CampusFix/issues/5), using the [HOD interview guide](https://github.com/Dhruvivek/CampusFix/blob/interview-guides/docs/questionnaire/hod-interview-guide.md).

**Source:** the author answered as the HOD on 2026-10-26. This was not a recorded conversation with the HOD, so treat these as working assumptions until they are checked with the HOD in person.

### How a repair request is handled today

- A student writes an application. Staff first check by hand whether the problem is real and whether it is serious, or can wait a week or so. This check is described as tiring because the application carries no proof.
- The carpenter is **in-house**.
- Management is informed sometimes, but usually not. No separate spending approval was mentioned.
- **Why it's written:** so both the person who writes the application and the person who receives it are accountable for it.

### Proof

- **A photo or video** is what's wanted. The campus is big and nobody has time to inspect every problem in person.
- **Fake complaints:** small ones are ignored. If one turns out to be a big prank, the student who wrote the application is punished.

### Hearing about new reports

- What they'd want is a **dashboard showing this week's reported problems**.
- Managing many separate applications is hard.
- **Repairs are batched.** The college won't call a carpenter, plumber and so on for every small problem each day. Tradespeople come about **once a week** and fix that week's problems. They are called sooner only for emergencies.

### Status updates

- Willing to update statuses if it takes **one or two clicks**. There's no time to edit details on each problem one at a time.
- Wanted: **update many problems at once**, and/or keep to **two or three simple statuses**. The example given was opening the dashboard and marking "called the carpenter".

### Places and handing off to the carpenter

- Wanted: **filter problems of the same type, then export or print the list** and give it to the carpenter, who works through it one by one.
- **Not answered:** how a location is described today (room numbers, blocks, seat rows) and whether a room register exists.

### Environment

- **College email:** Microsoft.
- **Grievance or complaints portal:** none.

### Gaps to fill in a real interview

- Q5: how rooms and seats are named today, and whether a list or register of rooms exists.
- Q8: a walk-through of the last repair arranged: how they heard, who did what, how long it took, how the student found out.
- Who besides the HOD would update statuses, if anyone.
- Confirm all of the above with the HOD directly.

### Implications to test (author's reading, not the HOD's words)

- A report probably needs photo or video evidence attached to cut down on manual checks.
- The staff view should be a weekly batch view more than a per-report alert stream. That matches how repairs are actually scheduled.
- Status changes need to be bulk and minimal, with very few states.
- A filtered, printable list for each trade (carpenter, plumber, …) may matter more than any notification channel.
- A Microsoft email setup suggests Microsoft sign-in is the natural fit if staff or students log in with college accounts.
- With no grievance portal, there's nothing existing to integrate with or compete against.
