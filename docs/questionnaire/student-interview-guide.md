# Student Interview Guide

About 10 minutes, in person, between classes. One student at a time. Use a fresh copy of the [notes template](interview-notes-template.md) for each student.

## Rules for you

- Ask about **the last time**, not "would you…". Past behaviour is evidence; predictions are not.
- Do **not** mention CampusFix, an app, or a website until Part 5.
- Don't suggest answers. If they stall, stay quiet for a few seconds, then ask "What else?"
- Use whatever language they're comfortable in. Write quotes in that language and add the English beside them.
- Follow-ups that always work: "Tell me more about that." · "What happened next?" · "Why?"

## Part 0 — Consent (30 seconds)

> "I'm doing a course project on how problems in the department get noticed and fixed. Can I ask you a few questions for about ten minutes? I won't write your name down. I may quote what you say in my project, but without your name. Is it OK if I record audio on my phone? I'll delete it once I've written up my notes."

- Recording OK → start recording. Not OK → take notes by hand.
- Note their year and section only, and give them a number: S1, S2, …

## Part 1 — Broad opener (2 minutes)

1. "In the last month, what on campus has been broken or annoying?"
   - Write down **everything**, in the order they say it. Don't prompt with examples.
   - "Anything else?" (ask once more)

## Part 2 — The classroom (2 minutes)

2. "Tell me about the last time something in a **classroom** was broken or not working."
   - What was it? Where? When was this, roughly?
   - "How did it affect you?" (e.g. stood, moved seat, couldn't see the board)
3. "What did you do about it?"
4. "Have you ever reported a problem like this to anyone?"
   - **Yes** → go to Part 3A. **No** → go to Part 3B.

## Part 3A — They have reported (3 minutes)

5. "Walk me through the last time you reported something. Who did you tell, and how?"
6. "How long did that take you?"
7. "What happened afterwards?" — Was it fixed? How long did it take? How did you find out?
8. "Did you report anything after that? Why, or why not?"
9. "After you reported it, what did you wonder about?"

## Part 3B — They have not reported (3 minutes)

5. "What stopped you from reporting it?"
6. "Who would you have told, if you had?" — Do they know who's responsible?
7. "Has anyone you know reported something? What happened?"
8. "What would have made reporting it worth it?"

## Part 4 — Wrap-up of behaviour (30 seconds)

10. "Is there anything about broken things in classrooms I should have asked and didn't?"

## Part 5 — The reveal (1–2 minutes)

Draw a line in your notes here. Everything below is **opinion**, not finding.

> "One idea I'm considering: you'd report a problem from your phone, logging in with your college email, and you'd add a photo. The HOD would see your name on it."

11. "What's your first reaction?"
12. "Do you use your college email? Is it a Google account, a Microsoft account, or something else?"
13. "How would you feel about your name being on the report?" — Any worry about being blamed for the damage?
14. "How would you feel about taking a photo of the problem?"

> "Thank you, that's really helpful."
